## 14.1 Product boundaries

`context_engine` is an optional, extractable in-process Python package. It receives immutable sanitized ordinary turns, exact host-issued execution/approval projections, strategy parameters, limits, revision identity and an injected maintenance callback. It owns derived views and checkpoints only. It imports no Optimus host, Gateway, security, evidence-handoff, workspace, Redis or provider implementation. Verified by import inspection at 91fd64a: `src/context_engine` imports only the standard library, so the zero-dependency property belongs to `context_engine` alone; `optimus_model_policy` additionally imports PyYAML and pydantic.

Optimus owns canonical storage, current prompt, exact authority, turn lifecycle, current workspace resolution, policy capture, model-facing assembly and notices. `optimus_model_policy` is neutral shared policy code; host and Gateway independently consume the trusted immutable snapshot. The Gateway alone performs approved upstream calls and enforces final request capacity for each actual attempt. Existing ledger/telemetry owners account for every attempt.

The local Gateway, shared-secret boundary, independent client-MCP trust controls, public tool dependencies and OTel/OTLP topology remain as specified in earlier sections. Plan 12 adds no Gateway MCP broker, provider key in CORE or generalized plugin platform.

## 14.2 Canonical source and selected views

Without the engine, retain full canonical history and inclusive 524,288-byte admission, its 80% storage warning, and the already accepted history-order/display exceptions. An attached engine may use the released offline 1,048,576-byte source class; production attachment still requires its own activation. Source size is distinct from provider request capacity and the context meter. A prompt-time 131,072-byte record reservation is not a cap on the eventual reply or entire turn.

Selection and packing are deterministic; only the summary text is generated. Compaction and Hybrid invoke the injected summarizer callback; Sliding never does. Compaction is the default. It retains a newest whole-turn exact tail and summarizes earlier ordinary history. Hybrid optionally retains the first complete turn as an exact anchor, uses a larger exact tail and summarizes the middle; deduplicate overlapping head/tail turns. Sliding takes the newest complete turns that fit, makes zero summary calls and does not skip an oversized newest turn to recover older ordinary history. All three retain exact protected state. Derived omission never deletes canonical history.

The proposed cheap/ultra-cheap history allowance is the smaller of 131,072 tokens and 80% of usable input, further constrained by final packing after protected state. It is a ceiling on history sent, not a threshold that delays summarization. The released offline summary cap is 8,192 tokens; compaction tail is 32,768 tokens, hybrid anchor 16,384 tokens and hybrid tail 65,536 tokens. Thus compaction's ordinary tail plus summary is at most 40,960 tokens; hybrid's head, tail and summary at most 90,112, before exact protected facts and other material. These are adopted offline test values carried by the reviewed test profiles and fixtures (for example `optimus_model_policy/test_profile_qualification.yaml` output reserves 32768/8192), not shipped production defaults; production defaults and route activation remain held.

Whole-turn summarization remains the simplest accepted design direction. If an indivisible older turn cannot fit maintenance, report the reason and the filed recovery choices; do not split it, silently switch strategies or discard ordinary text inside compaction/hybrid. Sliding can omit even all ordinary history when its newest turn does not fit. Any unavailable view at <=524288 projected source bytes may use full-history fallback when its complete final request fits, including an oversized turn or missing summarizer. Above that floor it refuses readably with OPEN disposition; the refusal is recoverable and introduces no monetary latch or new fallback mechanism. Availability depends on the actual history, estimator and bounds. Within-turn splitting and delayed-trigger/call-frequency optimization remain deferred.

## 14.3 Authority, selection and continuity

Model-authored history and generated summaries are inert. A claim that a change ran, a test passed or an approval exists cannot create host authority. Preserve every committed turn's exact outcome/effect projection and host-issued approval facts, independently of ordinary history selection. Historical approval describes an earlier decision and grants no fresh execution.

Exact retained earlier plans/replies may supply ordinary file/skill selection hints. Resolve file references against the current eligible workspace and assemble current bounded content. A resolved path does not verify an old diagnosis. Generated summary text does not directly select files or skills. A reference appearing only in omitted history may be unavailable to selection; do not claim full-history retrieval after compaction.

Model changes preserve canonical conversation and the strategy-defined view. They do not trigger a paid whole-chat factual audit. Use existing targeted freshness checks for selected file data and controlled edit targets. Preserve approval/version checks; no background watcher, durable claim database, semantic pinning or repository-wide rescan is introduced. These controls do not eliminate races with unrelated external writers.

## 14.4 Capacity and policy

Every final model request uses `effective_total = min(262144, verified_route_window)`, `usable_input = effective_total - verified_output_reserve`, and a complete-input estimate that includes messages, templates, workspace/tool/evidence material and framing. Admit equality; count the actual enforced output cap once, including verified reasoning semantics. Recompute at the final Gateway boundary for every actual provider attempt. When a model has several permitted endpoints, the applicable smallest verified capacity governs. The estimator is the admitted route's byte-ratio profile (`ceil(r * UTF8_bytes)` with non-negative overhead), not exact tokenization. At `bf440cb`, ADR-025 permits an explicitly recorded per-model empirical admission with `verified: false`; `conservative-utf8` uses one token per UTF-8 byte of message/tool text, 16 tokens per message and 256 fixed. Those allowances are assumptions checked against each reply's reported input usage, not a proven universal token or framing bound. A pre-call refusal dispatches no upstream attempt for that rejected request and remains readable/recoverable; earlier maintenance attempts can still have receipts.

D7's proposed named exception retains inclusive 524288-byte canonical storage/admission and its warning, but does not guarantee that full history fits a provider request. Both absent-engine and attached full-history fallback still undergo the final complete-request guard; overflow gives the accepted readable OPEN refusal without answer/planning dispatch. Do not lower the source cap or exempt capacity. The release implements this disclosed exception offline; its verbatim D7 record is filed in ADR-018 from registered ADR-011 source S4 (decision 2026-10-04, filed 2026-10-07). Exact per-route tokenizers remain deferred; route-specific byte-ratio safety requires normalization/pre-tokenization/framing proof and cannot be inferred from samples. Unknown route facts remain ineligible except for the explicit per-entry admissions ADR-025 permits. An empirical admission records its evidence, assumptions and limitations; it does not relabel unverified facts as verified. A first call can exceed its estimate or incur a charge before the reply check stops later calls to that model.

## 14.7 Auto mode: opt-in per-turn routing

**Launch boundary.** Only `OPTIMUS_AGENT_MODEL=auto` selects Auto. `--model auto` and an explicit model conflicting with that environment selection refuse. Auto composes packaged defaults, `auto_overlay.yaml`, then the trusted operator override. Approval binds both the effective registry literal and `_composition=auto`; ordinary and Auto approvals are not interchangeable, even if policy hashes coincide. A manually managed Auto Gateway is launched with `optimus-trust --workspace-root <workspace> run-gateway --auto`, under a durable approval binding that composition.

**Selection.** Before planning or answering, the host freezes a bounded sanitized prompt excerpt, captured mode, attachment metadata and the last committed routed tier. H applies its frozen rules; at rule confidence at least 0.8 it uses their answer, otherwise it asks the configured classifier through the Gateway. A valid answer below 0.95 moves up one tier, capped at complex. Missing/malformed confidence, abstention or classifier failure takes the failure rule, starting resolution at complex. These are routing heuristics, not calibrated safety guarantees.

The resolver selects the cheapest eligible unstopped model in the resulting task tier: easy, medium or complex. It tries another eligible model in the same role before moving upward when that role is unavailable; after exhausting tiers it tries the unstopped default planner, otherwise refuses recoverably without an answer call. The default fallback can be an easy or medium model. The chosen planning/answer model, route and context limits remain bound except for ADR-029's one recovery move (§14.8). After a grant, subsequent planning and that turn's approved application use the moved-to model and route; applying the plan does not classify it again. Maintenance retains the separately selected summarizer role.

**Shipped configuration.** Without an operator override, the Auto composition admits Luna then Haiku for easy, Gemini 3.8 Flash for medium/complex/escalation, Jev 1.14 as classifier and Haiku 5.5 as summarizer. These are registry assignments, not model-specific logic or a guarantee that a model can solve a task. At `5977155`, the escalation role supplies ADR-029's rung-2 target in interactive Auto planning. These shipped assignments do not guarantee a stronger available step for every tier. The ordinary composition does not acquire the Auto overlay.

**Calls and visibility.** Jev uses authenticated `POST /v1/decisions` and the Gateway's System One adapter. The request has its own admission/reply checks and one upstream attempt with no resend. System One supplies no finish status or router-attempt/pipeline evidence; those absent checks remain disclosed. Every classifier attempt enters the turn's `classification` settlement stage; known costs remain charged and unknown costs remain unknown. An accounting-integrity failure refuses the turn instead of entering the routing-failure fallback. Cancellation prevents subsequent answer dispatch while retaining incurred receipts.

The host attempts one informational `Auto: <tier> → <model>` notice before the routed planning/answer work and emits a content-free `auto_routing` event at most once per admitted turn. The record distinguishes routed, refused, cancelled and abandoned outcomes and records whether the notice was delivered. Notice and telemetry failures are contained; neither is a durable-delivery guarantee. The Auto host shares one monotonically growing model-stop set across its resolver, runners and summarizer; the Gateway independently enforces its per-model stops.

**Authority and limits.** ADR-028 accepts ADR-007 with H; the frozen ADR-007 is unchanged. The accepted pilot does not establish population error rates below 5%, and its previous-tier signal was barely tested (#425). Savings depend on the configured models and workload. A different judge mechanism has separate ownership (#426). ADR-029 accepts ADR-009 rung 2 as scoped in §14.8; #397 retains the evidence and documentation follow-ups. Auto grants no mutation authority and bypasses no exact-plan approval, guard, capacity or accounting control. §15 distinguishes source inspection, offline tests, pilot evidence and the three reported live slices.

## 14.8 One recovery escalation in interactive Auto planning

**At main `5977155` (ADR-029).** ADR-024 supplies the recovery episode: detection, one corrective nudge and the Continue/Stop check-in. In an interactive Auto session, a second detection, an allowance ending without a plan or refusal after the nudge, or a REFUSE settled after the nudge may grant one move before that check-in. A first refusal outside an episode remains a refusal; a plan produced after the nudge goes directly to the check-in. Reaching the planning limit without a detection does not itself trigger escalation.

The graph has one possible edge from the current implementer to the trusted registry's `escalation` role's first routable model. That role undergoes implementer eligibility checks. Later entries substitute for stopped earlier entries; they are not further rungs. No step exists if no target is routable, the current model is already the target, or the target has less usable input. The check-in reports when no stronger eligible model was available. Fixed-model launches, headless callers and `OPTIMUS_LOOP_RECOVERY=headless` retain their existing behavior.

A grant permits at most one move per episode and per turn. It gives the target Continue's allowance: three planning steps and a fresh 30 minutes by default. The episode remains open and proceeds to its check-in unless a halt or typed failure ends the run. The detector restarts for the target; later Continue episodes cannot grant another move in the same turn. Nothing runs while the user answers the check-in. Continue grants planning work or presents a waiting plan for its separate exact approval; it never approves a change.

Before switching, the host attempts a live notice identifying the move, its cause and the registry's input/output prices per million tokens, including the former prices. Delivery is recorded and does not gate the move; a halt wins. Contributor disclosure remains mandatory per request. Model identity, routing and target limits move together; captured history, strategy parameters, summarizer, maintenance/repack allowances and earlier receipts remain. The approved application uses the moved-to model. The initial `auto_routing` record stays the classifier's selection; `loop_recovery` records the escalation decision and, on a grant, `from_model`, `to_model` and `notice_delivered`. Each request keeps the route that sent it and its actual cost or explicit unknown.

The target is configuration, not a hard-coded model or a promise of task success. Escalation bypasses no approval, guard, disclosure, capacity or accounting check. Contracts and executable checks are in LLD §13.11; §15 qualifies their reported evidence.

## 14.9 Host/Gateway timing boundary

At `5977155`, the shared defaults are a 60-second upstream-attempt timeout, a 120-second upstream-dispatch budget per host request, and a 135-second host wait (`MAX_ROUTE_ATTEMPTS` × 60 seconds, plus 15 seconds of processing slack). This budget is time, not a product dollar stop. The Gateway starts a request-scoped deadline and checks that a complete attempt timeout still fits before every dispatch, including after a retry backoff. The two-attempt completion ceiling is a maximum, not an entitlement to a second attempt; the decisions route still makes at most one attempt without resend.

Each production upstream attempt is waited for through a worker-thread deadline. The Gateway abandons it at expiry, shuts down an available socket and refuses a late connection before request bytes leave. Uninterruptible setup may leave a worker running after the waiter returns; a 32-slot process-wide cap bounds outstanding workers, and a slot is held until its worker exits. `attempt_deadline`, `attempt_capacity` and `request_budget` distinguish the failures. The attempt's delivery classification remains separate: uncertain delivery/cost is never silently converted to zero or automatically resent under the enforced completion policy.

The 120-second budget controls upstream dispatch; it is not an end-to-end deadline on request reading, Stage C storage, logging or reply writing. Those operations have no whole-request deadline from this mechanism and can consume the host's slack. The 135-second default depends on responsive Gateway processing and storage. A shorter positive finite `--gateway-timeout-seconds` is allowed with a warning and can lose a billed reply; planning then retains its unknown-cost stop. A departed host's broken/reset reply socket produces a sanitized `reply_undeliverable` record. Prompt cancellation of an in-flight Gateway call and an end-to-end Gateway-processing deadline remain owed in #397. LLD §13.12 specifies the transport details.
